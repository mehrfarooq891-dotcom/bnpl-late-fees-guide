BNPL Late Fees Explained — Klarna, Afterpay & Affirm

Buy now, pay later feels free right up until a payment date slips past you. Each provider handles a missed payment differently, and if you run plans on two or three apps at once, the dates — not the amounts — are what usually catch you out.

Klarna late fees

Klarna charges a late fee when a scheduled Pay in 4 or Pay in 30 payment is missed, and a failed payment can also pause your spending power until the balance is settled. The exact fee depends on your order total and where you live, so the figure that matters is the one inside your own Klarna app — our full guide lists the current published amounts.

Afterpay late fees

Afterpay also charges late fees on missed instalments, but they are capped — an initial fee, a further fee if the instalment stays unpaid for another week, and a total cap tied to a percentage of the order value. The cap protects you from runaway fees, not from the first ones.

Affirm late fees

Affirm's published position is different: it does not charge late fees. That does not make a missed Affirm payment free — interest-bearing plans keep accruing interest, and missed payments can be reported and can affect your ability to borrow later.

Why stacked plans make late fees likely

One plan is easy to remember. Three plans across three apps means twelve or more payment dates a month, each set by the day you bought something — not by the day you get paid. When two or three instalments land in the days just before payday, one thin week is all it takes to miss one and start paying fees on money you already had.

The fix is a single calendar. Put every instalment from every app on one payday-aligned calendar and you can see the tight weeks before they happen — and move your spending, not your payment dates, to match.
